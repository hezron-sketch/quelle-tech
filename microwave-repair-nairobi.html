a) Microwave Not Heating

This is the most common microwave complaint in Nairobi. The microwave runs — you can hear the turntable spinning and the light comes on — but food comes out cold. This is almost always an internal component failure, not a wiring issue.

#CauseFix
1Magnetron failed — the core component that generates microwavesTest and replace magnetron. Most common cause in older units.
2High-voltage diode burnt outTest with multimeter, replace diode — inexpensive and quick fix
3High-voltage capacitor faultyDischarge safely, test, and replace capacitor
4Thermal cutout / thermoprotector blownReplace the thermal fuse — caused by overheating or poor ventilation
5Door interlock switch failedReplace door switch — microwave won't heat if it thinks door is open
⚠️ Never open a microwave yourself. The high-voltage capacitor stores a lethal charge even when the unit is unplugged. Always call a trained technician.

b) Microwave Sparking Inside

Sparking inside the microwave cavity is alarming — and means you should stop using the unit immediately. It is a fire and safety risk.

#CauseFix
1Damaged or burnt waveguide cover (the cardboard/mica sheet on the cavity wall)Replace the waveguide cover — cheap and quick, often same-day
2Metallic food residue or grease buildup inside cavityDeep clean the cavity; remove all burnt residue
3Failing magnetron arcing internallyReplace magnetron
4Damaged microwave rack or shelf with exposed metalReplace microwave rack with the correct type
⚠️ Stop using immediately. A sparking microwave is a fire hazard. Unplug it and call 0742 245720 before using it again.

c) Turntable Not Spinning

The food doesn't rotate during cooking, leading to uneven heating. This is usually a mechanical fault and one of the simpler repairs.

  • Turntable motor burnt — the small motor beneath the plate has failed; we replace it on the spot
  • Broken turntable coupling / drive bushing — the plastic coupling that connects motor to tray has cracked; inexpensive replacement
  • Broken turntable ring or support roller — the roller guide under the glass plate is cracked or jammed; replace the ring assembly
  • Misaligned or cracked glass turntable plate — replace glass plate with correct size for your model

d) Door Not Closing or Latch Broken

A microwave will not run — and should not run — if the door does not close and latch correctly. The door safety switches prevent operation with an open door.

  • Broken door latch hook — the plastic hooks that catch into the latch have snapped; replace door latch assembly
  • Faulty door interlock switch — one or more of the 2–3 safety switches behind the latch have failed; replace switches
  • Damaged door hinge — door doesn't align properly; hinge replacement or door realignment
  • Cracked door — if the door inner glass or seal is damaged, the unit must not be used until repaired
ℹ️ Most microwaves have 2–3 interlock switches. If even one fails, the unit will not operate. We test all switches on every repair call.

e) Display Not Working / Buttons Not Responding

  • Blown main fuse — usually caused by a power surge; replace the fuse (common after Kenya power fluctuations)
  • Faulty control board — the main circuit board has failed; check for burnt components, replace if necessary
  • Membrane keypad worn out — buttons become unresponsive after years of use; replace the keypad panel
  • Loose ribbon cable — the cable connecting keypad to control board has come loose; reseat or replace
⚡ Power surge tip: Always use a surge protector for your microwave. Kenya's power fluctuations frequently blow the internal fuse and damage control boards in Samsung, LG, and Panasonic units.

f) Microwave Making Loud Noise

  • Loud humming — magnetron or high-voltage transformer failing; needs inspection
  • Rattling — loose turntable ring or broken roller; replace roller assembly
  • Grinding — turntable motor failing; replace motor
  • Buzzing during operation — normal at low levels; excessive buzzing indicates a diode or magnetron issue

g) Microwave Trips the Power / Blows Fuse at Home

If your microwave causes the circuit breaker to trip every time it's used, this indicates a serious internal short circuit.

  • Shorted high-voltage capacitor — most common cause; capacitor has internally shorted to earth
  • Shorted magnetron — magnetron filament or antenna has shorted; replace magnetron
  • Damaged power cord — insulation breakdown in the power cable; replace cord
⚠️ Do not keep resetting the breaker and trying again. A microwave that trips the power has a dangerous internal fault. Unplug immediately and call us.

h) Smoke or Burning Smell

  • Burnt food residue inside cavity — deep clean required; grease buildup can ignite
  • Burnt waveguide cover — the mica sheet absorbs grease and begins to burn; replace it
  • Failing component on control board — visible burnt marks on PCB; component-level repair or board replacement
  • Burning magnetron — magnetron filament burning out; replacement needed

3. How a Microwave Oven Works

Understanding the basic mechanics helps explain why certain faults occur and why some repairs require specialist handling.

A microwave converts electrical energy into microwave radiation using a component called the magnetron. The magnetron requires very high voltage — typically 2,000–4,000 volts — generated by a high-voltage transformer, capacitor, and diode circuit. This high voltage is what makes microwaves dangerous to open without training.

Microwaves enter the food cavity through the waveguide and cause water molecules in the food to vibrate rapidly, generating heat. The turntable rotates food so it heats evenly. The door interlock switches (usually 2–3 switches) cut power to the magnetron the instant the door opens.

ℹ️ The high-voltage capacitor stores a charge of up to 4,000 volts and can deliver a fatal electric shock — even when the microwave is unplugged and switched off. This is why microwave repair must only be done by trained technicians.

5. How We Diagnose Your Microwave

Our technicians follow a structured process to find the fault accurately and safely.

  1. Visual InspectionCheck for obvious damage — burnt marks, cracked door, damaged cavity, burnt waveguide cover.
  2. Power CheckConfirm 240V supply, check internal fuse, test door interlock switches.
  3. Safety DischargeDischarge the high-voltage capacitor safely before touching any internal components. Non-negotiable step.
  4. Component TestingTest magnetron, diode, capacitor, and transformer using a multimeter and specialist tools.
  5. Control Board InspectionCheck PCB for burnt traces, failed relays, or damaged components.
  6. Turntable & Mechanical CheckTest turntable motor, coupling, and roller assembly for wear or breakage.
  7. Function TestRun a controlled heat test after repair to confirm the fault is fully resolved before leaving.

7. Tips to Extend Your Microwave's Life

  • Clean the cavity after every use. Grease and food splatter absorb microwave energy, burn onto the cavity walls, and eventually damage the waveguide cover and magnetron.
  • Replace the waveguide cover if it looks discoloured or burnt. It costs very little and prevents expensive magnetron damage.
  • Never run the microwave empty. With no food to absorb the microwaves, energy reflects back and destroys the magnetron within seconds.
  • Always ensure adequate ventilation. Leave at least 15cm clearance on all sides and above. Blocked vents cause thermal cutouts to blow repeatedly.
  • Use a surge protector. Kenya's power fluctuations frequently blow the internal fuse and damage control boards.
  • Never use metal containers or aluminium foil unless your unit specifically supports it. Metal causes sparking and damages the cavity.
  • Avoid slamming the door. The door latch hooks are plastic — repeated slamming cracks them over time.

8. Microwave Safety Warnings

⚡ Never open a microwave yourself. The high-voltage capacitor can hold a charge of up to 4,000 volts for hours after the unit is unplugged. This charge is fatal.
🔥 If your microwave is sparking or smoking — stop it immediately, unplug it, and do not use it again until it has been inspected. Call 0742 245720.
🚫 Never run a microwave with a damaged door seal or cracked door. Microwave radiation leakage is invisible and dangerous.
🔌 Never bypass a door interlock switch. These switches cut power to the magnetron when the door opens. Bypassing them allows microwave radiation to escape while the unit is operating.
