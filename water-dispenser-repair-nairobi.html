2. Common Water Dispenser Faults & How We Fix Them

Here are the most common problems we repair across Nairobi, along with the causes and how each is resolved.

a) Water Dispenser Not Cooling

Cold water that comes out warm or at room temperature is the most common complaint we receive. In most cases, the fix is straightforward.

#CauseFix
1Thermostat failed — should close below 5°CTest continuity, replace thermostat
2Compressor overload / relay clicking then silentReplace relay kit on the spot
3Gas leak / low refrigerant — warm condenserCheck oil residue at joints, regas
4Fan motor dead — no airflow over condenserReplace fan motor
🔧 Quick test: Feel the hot discharge line. If it's hot but the condenser is cold, the compressor is running but refrigerant isn't moving — indicating a gas leak.

b) No Hot Water

The hot tap runs cold or lukewarm. This is almost always an electrical component failure in the hot tank.

#CauseFix
1Heating element burnt out — open circuit on 500W elementTest with multimeter, replace element
2Thermal fuse blown — safety cutout has trippedReplace fuse — never bypass it
3Thermostat stuck open — won't send power to elementTest and replace thermostat
⚠️ Safety: Hot tanks operate at 85–95°C. We always power off and drain the hot tank before removing any component. Bypassing a thermal fuse is a fire hazard — never do it.

c) Water Dispenser Leaking

Water pooling under the unit, around the base, or from the drip tray. The cause differs between top load and bottom load units.

Top Load units:

  • Cracked water bottle — inspect for hairline cracks around the neck
  • Damaged probe O-ring — we carry these on every service call
  • Reservoir overflow — blocked drain or faulty float valve; common with Nairobi's hard water

Bottom Load units:

  • Cracked pump housing — replace pump assembly (very common in Hisense, Ramtons, Bruhm)
  • Loose internal silicone tubes — re-clamp or replace; yellow/slimy tubes need full replacement
  • Faulty solenoid valve on cold tank — replace when it fails to seal

Drip tray overflowing:

  • Kinked or blocked drain tube — common in Mika, Kenwood, and Hotpoint; we straighten or replace on every visit

d) No Water Flow or Slow Flow

Taps that run dry or barely produce a trickle. Most common in bottom load units.

#CauseFix
1Pump motor burnt — no sound, no vibrationTest winding resistance, replace pump
2Float sensor stuck — pump thinks tank is fullClean sensor to restore normal operation
3Control board failure — no voltage to pumpInspect for burnt traces, replace board
4Air lock — water path blocked by trapped airRun pump with outlet open to prime; remove bottle, press both taps, re-insert
5Blocked faucet — mineral deposits clogging flowClean or replace faucet tap

e) Bad Taste, Smell, Milky Water or Moulds

Water that looks, tastes, or smells off is a hygiene issue and should be addressed immediately.

  • Bacterial growth in reservoir — full sanitization required. We drain, clean, and flush all water paths with food-grade sanitizer.
  • Moulds and algae buildup — deep clean and disinfection. Contaminated silicone tubes are replaced.
  • Old or degraded silicone tubing — yellow or slimy tubes leach odour into water. Full replacement is the only permanent fix.
☀️ Never place the unit in direct sunlight. Heat accelerates bacterial and mould growth inside the reservoir significantly.

f) Broken or Dripping Tap / Faucet

  • Cracked faucet — replaced with a matching compatible part on the spot
  • Worn valve or seat — causes constant dripping; we replace the valve seat or full tap assembly
  • Scale blocking the tap — mineral deposits prevent full open/close; descaling or tap replacement

g) Noisy Operation

  • Buzzing — loose compressor mounts or fan blade hitting casing; tighten mounts, adjust fan guard
  • Excessive gurgling — indicates low refrigerant; unit needs regassing
  • Screaming or boiling sound — hot tank scale buildup causing dangerous overheating; descale immediately

3. How Top Load & Bottom Load Dispensers Work

Understanding your dispenser helps explain why certain faults happen.

Top Load: Gravity-fed. The bottle sits upside down on a probe and water flows down into separate cold and hot reservoirs. The cold tank uses an evaporator coil; the hot tank uses a 500W heating element. Thermostats control both. Simpler design means fewer failure points.

Bottom Load: An internal pump draws water from a bottle stored in the cabinet up to the reservoir. It uses a float sensor and a pump control board. More components means more things that can go wrong — pumps, float switches, control boards, and solenoid valves can all fail independently.

5. How We Diagnose Your Dispenser

Every Quelle Technologies technician follows the same 6-step process — no guesswork.

  1. Power CheckVerify 240V at socket and check the fuse. Many "dead" units just have a blown fuse.
  2. ListenCompressor running? Pump running? Clicking or silence? Audio clues cut diagnosis time significantly.
  3. Check TemperaturesHot tank should be warm. Cold tank should be sweating condensation. Off readings confirm thermostat or refrigerant issues.
  4. Trace the Water PathIs water reaching the reservoir? Check probe, pump, float sensor — common source of "no water" complaints.
  5. Electrical TestingMultimeter tests on elements, thermostats, and thermal fuses confirm which components have failed.
  6. Leak TestDry unit completely, run for 10 minutes, inspect for fresh moisture. Confirms leak source before closing up.

7. Tips to Extend Your Dispenser's Life

  • Replace thermostats in pairs. If one fails, replace both hot and cold — they age at the same rate and the second will follow shortly.
  • Never run the unit empty. Turning it on without water burns out the heating element in as little as 60 seconds.
  • Use a surge protector. Kenya's frequent power surges kill control boards — especially in Samsung and LG models. A protector costs far less than a board replacement.
  • Avoid direct sunlight. Heat accelerates bacterial and mould growth in the reservoir and forces the compressor to overwork.
  • Descale regularly if on borehole water. Hard water in Nairobi builds scale inside heating elements every 2 months — descaling prevents burnout.
  • Clean faucets weekly. Wipe taps regularly; mineral deposits and dirt accumulate quickly and block flow over time.

8. Safety Reminders

🔌 Always disconnect power before opening any panel — never work on a live unit.
🌡️ Hot tanks reach 85–95°C — always drain the hot tank fully before removing it or any connected component.
🔥 Never bypass thermal fuses — they are fire-prevention safety devices. Always replace with the correct rated fuse.
💨 Suspected gas leak? Check for oil residue around joints. Do not use open flame or create sparks near the unit.
