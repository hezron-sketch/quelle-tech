a) Washing Machine Not Spinning

The drum fills and washes but clothes come out soaking wet — the spin cycle is not engaging or completing. This is one of the most common faults we see in Nairobi.

#CauseFix
1Worn or snapped drive belt (top load)Replace drive belt — most common cause in top-loaders
2Failed lid switch / door latch interlockMachine won't spin if it thinks lid/door is open; replace switch or latch
3Overloaded drumRemove some items; never fill drum more than ¾ full
4Worn motor brushesCarbon brushes wear down over time; replace brushes — inexpensive fix
5Failed motor control boardTest control board; replace if motor control circuit has failed
6Bearing failureLoud grinding during spin; replace drum bearings — common in machines over 5 years
🔧 Quick check: If the machine washes but won't spin, try opening and firmly closing the lid/door before starting again. A door latch not fully engaging is a very common cause of spin failure.

b) Washing Machine Not Draining

Water left sitting in the drum at the end of a cycle. This can also cause the machine to stop mid-cycle or refuse to spin.

#CauseFix
1Blocked drain pump filter — coins, lint, small items trappedClean the drain pump filter (usually behind a small panel at the bottom front of the machine)
2Kinked or clogged drain hoseStraighten or clear hose; check the standpipe height is correct
3Failed drain pump motorTest pump; replace if motor has seized or burnt out
4Blocked coin trapClear coins, buttons, and debris from coin trap — very common cause
5Control board not triggering drain cycleBoard fault; diagnose and replace if drain signal is missing
💡 Before calling: Locate the drain pump filter (usually bottom front, behind a small flap) and check for coins, hair clips, or lint blockages. Clearing this takes 5 minutes and resolves most drain failures.

c) Washing Machine Leaking Water

Water on the floor is an urgent fault — it creates an electrical hazard and can damage flooring and cabinetry.

  • Damaged door seal / boot gasket (front load) — the rubber seal around the door has split or has debris pressed into it; clean or replace gasket
  • Loose or cracked water inlet hose — check both ends of the inlet hose at back of machine; tighten or replace
  • Faulty water inlet valve — valve not shutting fully after filling; replace valve
  • Cracked drum or tub — rare but serious; requires drum replacement
  • Overflow due to too much detergent — excessive foam can escape through the door seal; reduce detergent and use HE detergent for front loaders
  • Worn drum bearings causing seal damage — when bearings fail, the drum wobbles and wears through the rear drum seal; replace bearings and seal
⚠️ Unplug the machine immediately if water is pooling near the base. Water near the motor, capacitor, or control board is a serious electrical hazard.

d) Washing Machine Won't Start

  • Power supply issue — check socket, fuse, and extension cable; try a different socket
  • Door / lid not fully closed — the interlock switch prevents starting if the door sensor detects it is open; close firmly and try again
  • Faulty door latch or interlock switch — switch has failed and is not sending the "door closed" signal; replace latch assembly
  • Burnt or failed control board — no response when buttons are pressed; board inspection and replacement
  • Water supply tap closed — machine fills before starting; ensure the water tap behind the machine is fully open
  • Child lock activated — many machines have a child lock; check the manual to deactivate

e) Washing Machine Shaking or Vibrating Violently

Violent vibration during spin damages the machine, walks across the floor, and can loosen plumbing connections. Almost always an easily fixable mechanical issue.

  • Machine not level — adjust the feet until machine is completely level on all four corners; use a spirit level
  • Transport bolts not removed — new machines have shipping bolts in the rear; these must be removed before first use
  • Unbalanced load — one heavy item (duvet, jeans) wraps to one side; redistribute load or wash heavy items with other items
  • Worn drum bearings — severe vibration and loud grinding/rumbling noise during spin; replace drum bearings
  • Broken or worn shock absorbers / suspension springs — dampers that cushion drum movement have failed; replace shock absorbers
  • Damaged drum paddle / lift — broken plastic paddle inside drum causes imbalance; replace paddle

f) Washing Machine Stops Mid-Cycle

  • Overheating motor — thermal cut-out trips; machine stops and restarts after cooling; clean motor vents and check for blocked filter
  • Water not draining between cycles — machine pauses waiting to drain; check drain pump and hose
  • Pressure switch fault — switch that detects water level is faulty; machine stops because it thinks tub is full or empty incorrectly
  • Control board fault — intermittent board failures cause random mid-cycle stops; diagnose and replace board
  • Power fluctuation — Kenya's unstable power causes machines to stop; use a voltage stabiliser
ℹ️ A voltage stabiliser is highly recommended for washing machines in Nairobi. Power fluctuations are a leading cause of control board failures and mid-cycle stops across all brands.

g) Drum Not Turning / Washing Machine Humming But Not Moving

  • Broken drive belt — motor runs (hum) but drum doesn't turn; replace belt — very common and inexpensive
  • Seized drum bearings — drum locked in place due to failed bearings; replace bearings
  • Failed motor start capacitor — motor hums but won't start rotating; replace capacitor
  • Jammed foreign object — coin or small item trapped between drum and tub; remove obstruction

h) Burning Smell from Washing Machine

  • Worn motor brushes burning — carbon brushes worn to the point of arcing; replace brushes immediately
  • Seized bearing causing friction burn — drum rubbing against drum seal or rear bearing housing; replace bearings
  • Belt slipping on seized drum — drive belt burning on a drum that won't turn freely; replace belt and fix drum
  • Electrical fault on control board — burnt component on PCB; inspect and replace board
⚠️ A burning smell means stop the machine immediately and unplug it. Continued operation risks an electrical fire. Call 0742 245720.

3. How a Washing Machine Works

Understanding the basics helps explain why faults occur and what our technicians look for during diagnosis.

When you start a cycle, the water inlet valve opens to fill the drum to a level detected by the pressure switch. The motor — via the drive belt and drum pulley — rotates the drum to agitate clothes with water and detergent. The heating element warms the water to the selected temperature, controlled by a thermostat. After washing, the drain pump removes water, and the motor spins the drum at high speed to remove moisture from clothes. All of this is timed and sequenced by the control board (or programmer on older models).

Front load machines tumble clothes through a small amount of water using gravity. Top load machines use an agitator or impeller to move clothes through a full tub of water. Twin tubs have separate mechanical wash and spin motors and require manual water management.

5. How We Diagnose Your Washing Machine

Our technicians follow a structured process — no guesswork, no unnecessary parts replaced.

  1. Visual InspectionCheck for visible damage — cracked hoses, worn belt (visible through back panel), water damage, burnt marks.
  2. Power & Safety CheckConfirm 240V supply, check door/lid interlock is engaging correctly, check all programme responses.
  3. Water Supply CheckConfirm tap is open, inlet hose is not kinked, and water pressure is adequate.
  4. Drain System CheckInspect and clean drain pump filter; check drain hose routing and standpipe height.
  5. Belt & Motor CheckInspect drive belt condition; test motor with multimeter; check carbon brush length.
  6. Drum & Bearing CheckRotate drum by hand — listen for rumbling (bearings) or feel for roughness; check for drum wobble.
  7. Electrical TestingTest heating element, thermostat, pressure switch, and control board components with multimeter.
  8. Full Cycle TestAfter repair, run a complete wash and spin cycle to confirm everything works before leaving.

8. Tips to Extend Your Washing Machine's Life

  • Never overload the drum. Fill no more than ¾ full. Overloading stresses bearings, shock absorbers, and the motor — the three most expensive components to replace.
  • Always check pockets before loading. Coins, keys, and hair clips in the drum damage the drum seal, block the drain pump, and scratch the drum interior.
  • Use the correct detergent amount. Front loaders require HE (low-foam) detergent. Too much detergent produces excess foam, damages door seals, and causes overflow.
  • Use a voltage stabiliser. Power fluctuations in Nairobi damage control boards — particularly in Samsung and LG machines. A stabiliser protects the electronics.
  • Leave the door open after a cycle. Especially for front loaders — this allows the drum and door seal to dry, preventing mould growth in the rubber gasket.
  • Level the machine correctly. An unlevel machine vibrates excessively during spin, accelerating wear on bearings, shock absorbers, and drum mounts. Use the adjustable feet.
  • Clean the drum monthly. Run an empty hot cycle (60°C or higher) with a washing machine cleaner. This removes detergent residue, bacteria, and limescale from the drum and hoses.
  • Don't ignore warning signs. Loud grinding during spin means bearings are failing. A burning smell means motor brushes are worn. Catching these early saves a much bigger repair bill.
